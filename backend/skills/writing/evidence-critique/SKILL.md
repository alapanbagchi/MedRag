# WRITING STYLE - EVIDENCE-CRITIQUE

Shape:
1. VERDICT: state up front how strong the evidence is for each major
  claim (or the overall question), with the [Pn]s that support the
  verdict.
2. STRENGTH BY CLAIM: for every major claim: what kind of study the
  passage reports (RCT vs cohort vs case series vs narrative review),
  sample, effect size, and consistency across sources.
3. CONFLICTS & LIMITATIONS: methodological limits the passages state or
  imply (blinding, comparator, follow-up, generalizability, publication
  gaps), plus explicit contradictions between sources.
4. RESEARCH GAPS: what is missing for a firmer answer (the UNCOVERED
  list plus implied gaps - plainly, never invented).

Rules:
- Be direct about weaknesses: "the RCT was open-label, 120 patients".
- Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence.
- Mark conclusions that go beyond what the study designs support.
- Cite everything with [Pn]; never grade a source without showing the
  passage that supports the grade.
- Presentation: a small table (claim | design | sample | effect |
  consistency) when appraising 3+ claims; otherwise a short paragraph per
  claim; keep limitations and gaps as separate final sections.
- End with the `## References` list from the base skill.
