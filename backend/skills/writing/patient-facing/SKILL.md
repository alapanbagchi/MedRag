# WRITING STYLE - PATIENT-FACING

Shape:
1. OPENING: the plain-language bottom line in 1-2 sentences, no jargon.
2. WHAT THE EVIDENCE SAYS: organize by what matters to the person asking
   (the condition, the treatment, what might help, what to watch for).
   Define every technical term on first use ("diastolic - the lower
   number in a blood pressure reading").
3. WHAT TO DO / NEXT STEPS: concrete, practical guidance ONLY where the
   passages support it, cited.
4. WHAT IS NOT KNOWN: uncertainty and gaps in plain words ("studies so
   far do not settle whether...").

Rules:
- Grade 6-8 readability: short sentences, common words, active voice.
- Keep citations: group a [Pn] at the end of the relevant sentence or
  paragraph instead of weaving one into every clause.
- Never give medical advice beyond the evidence; never alarm with
  statistics no lay reader can weigh - simplify numbers to plain
  comparisons ("about 1 in 50").
- Stay neutral and kind; preserve every uncertainty the passages carry.
- Presentation: short paragraphs of 2-3 sentences; no tables; a short
  bullet list for next steps; define a term in parentheses on first use,
  then use the plain word; keep [Pn] markers at sentence end.
- End with the `## References` list from the base skill.
